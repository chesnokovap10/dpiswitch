Direct routing

When off, everything goes through the tunnel and the detector only records verdicts. When a domain has 3+ verified direct subdomains and no blocked ones, new subdomains go direct right away without waiting for a check. Helps services with a pool of servers (speedtest, CDNs). The first blocked subdomain removes the rule. Provides IPv6 even if your ISP has none: it goes through the tunnel. Sites on the direct list stay on IPv4. Switching restarts the core. How long a site bypasses the tunnel before it is checked again. Blocks get lifted — no point keeping a site in the tunnel forever. A site that lost its direct path is re-checked less and less often — but no less often than this. How much slower than the tunnel the direct path may be. 0 — only if not slower. More attempts — a more accurate decision, but a longer check.

DNS

Queried directly, outside the tunnel — CDNs return nodes for your ISP. The detector uses it too, so it tests exactly the node traffic will go to. Prefer IPs over names.
For sites going through the tunnel: nodes are picked for the VPS. Empty — DNS from the .conf.

Formats: https://IP/dns-query (DoH), tls://IP (DoT), IP (plain DNS). One per line, queried concurrently. Many ISPs block plain DNS — prefer DoH. Changing DNS restarts the core: connectivity drops for a couple of seconds.
